Tonight, sitting out by Shenzhen Bay feeling the sea breeze, I was reading *Duku* (a Chinese long-form magazine). Out of nowhere, it hit me: I've never actually possessed any real ability to write.

How would I define that writing ability? To me, it means being able to actually *finish* an article—explaining a concept or a topic clearly enough that it reaches a publishable state, leaving the reader with something tangible to take away.

The simplest yardstick is length.

- 1k+ words (usually 2k+): Counts as a long post, which requires a certain amount of organizational skill. These typically live on platforms like blogs, personal websites, or WeChat Official Accounts.
- A few hundred to 1k words: Mostly focused on opinions and brief descriptions, typically posted on visual and microblogging platforms like Twitter, Weibo, or Xiaohongshu (a Chinese lifestyle and recommendation platform).

What I want most is to write long-form posts, but I've never actually sat down and done it in earnest. Over the past two years, I've habitually jotted down all kinds of diary entries, emotional vents, and fleeting little thoughts. But genre-wise, they've been far too fragmented and self-absorbed. Taken on its own, any single note could only ever be posted as a quick social update or stashed away as raw material for some future long piece.

And so, nothing was ever "finished," nothing was ever "published," and nothing ever brought that sense of "accomplishment."

Dragging my feet, day after day slipped away into endless delays and frustration.

So what should I do? Here's where my head is at right now:

- Keep journaling and capturing emotions as usual. They're the source, but I have to remember they aren't the end result.
- For long-form writing, build a topic backlog, run pieces like individual projects, and periodically review the "results."
- Believe that I'm just a temporarily down-on-his-luck genius writer.
