From a productivity standpoint, the past two months have been a total write-off. The draft I just deleted was nothing but self-reproach and guilty introspection. But then I caught myself: beating yourself up is the easiest thing in the world, and the most useless. Jotting a few things down, on the other hand, actually feels kind of nice.

## Web Novels

I ended up bingeing two web novels: *The Mirror Legacy* (*Xuanjian Xianzu*) and *High Martial Era* (*Gaowu Jiyuan*). I just checked my March spending on Qidian: 31 top-ups at 6 yuan each, totaling 186 yuan. That's basically more than half a year's usual budget.

*High Martial Era* is a total Frankenstein mashup. Early on it's basically *Swallowed Star*, then it turns into *Lord Xue Ying*, and in the later stages it's just standard fantasy tropes. Barely anything about it stuck with me.

*The Mirror Legacy*, on the other hand, was my first run-in with this particular subgenre: multi-generational clan cultivation with an ensemble cast. Even now, I can still name every key character across the first four generations of the Li family. From knowing nothing of the immortal path to a tiny clan scraping by in the cracks, grinding through four generations of sacrifice until they finally established themselves as a Zifu immortal family—it was hands down the most enjoyable web novel reading experience I've had in years. The shame is that later on, the cast grows increasingly unfamiliar and the scope sprawls out of control. Once it gets bogged down in the political scheming of high-level power brokers, it completely loses that singular magic from the early chapters, where the cultivation world was just slowly unfolding.

## Gaming

On the gaming side, I played *Split Fiction* with my girlfriend and picked *League of Legends: Wild Rift* back up.

We got through about four chapters of *Split Fiction* before visual and gameplay fatigue started setting in. The movement mechanics boil down to hopping on pillars, grappling hooks, chain jumping, and wall running. As for the minigames, they're the same old formulas reskinned with new textures and backgrounds.

What's harder to tolerate is the poor signposting. When you finally solve a puzzle only to realize it leads right into yet another cliché level gimmick, it gets genuinely frustrating. Moments like that always make me appreciate *The Legend of Zelda*'s design all over again: crystal-clear guidance, ingenious labyrinths, and that seamless promise that if you can see a spot, you can actually go there.

Returning to *Wild Rift* was born purely out of boredom. After weeks of reading web novels, playing games, and scrolling through social feeds, my brain had turned to mush. I just needed another way to kill time—and when you're that bored, the default move is falling back into an old game.

To be fair, the mobile version is well made. Farming, trading, and teamfighting all feel surprisingly dialed in, and I've been mildly hooked for the past few days. The only thing that really bugs me is how dependent the ADC role is on your teammates and the match state. It's so easy to come out of laning without an edge, never even get a chance to pop off, and then just watch your base get steamrolled in a single push. The frustration is real.

Then again, doesn't that align perfectly with the original PC game?

---

I keep telling myself that old line: "Go a day without reading, and your face turns repulsive." It really is true. Not only do I feel uglier, my brain feels distinctly duller, too.

So yeah—time to go read a book for a bit.
