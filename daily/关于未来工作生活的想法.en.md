Work was pretty miserable this week. I plan to talk to my boss on Monday about dropping this part of my workload. Staying at AC any longer feels like a constant test of my tolerance for work. Of course, an ideal job is hard to come by, so here's a revisit of my thoughts on work and the life ahead.

- Broadly speaking, pursuing personal freedom and holding down a good job run in parallel.
	- Getting current work done while exploring things on the side is the core strategy for self-fulfillment and hedging against instability.
	- But when it comes to building something of larger value, it takes a team or a company to scale it up.
- During the first six months of a job, you're full of excitement and curiosity about a new domain. After a year, exhaustion and boredom set in. I could never quite figure out where that boredom came from, so I can only chalk it up to structural issues with the very concept of "having a day job."
	- What's the remedy? Create. Build things that originate from you as an individual, carry your personal stamp, and bring value to others.
	- If you can meet market demand and generate some income, that actually strengthens your ability to withstand uncertainty.
	- Back in 2022, I hit an existential crisis while working a day job. I quit under the guise of "self-exploration"—only to sink hundreds of hours into video games, movies, and books, accompanied by non-stop anxiety. I can never use "exploration" as an excuse to quit rashly again.
- Yet individual capacity has its limits. Anyone who has tried shipping a product solo knows the massive chasm between product, design, engineering, and operations skill sets, not to mention how limited one person's energy is. Building something of greater value definitely requires an exceptional team and great partners.
	- Accept where my capabilities fall short right now, take care of what's in front of me, and actively look for a better job and team.
	- Keep sharpening my skills so when the right opportunity comes along, I can catch it.

Lately, whenever I think through specific tasks, it's easy to spiral into anxiety. There always seem to be too many things I could do and should do, and doing something halfway feels as pointless as doing nothing at all. It reminded me of something Suigua (an indie developer) once said. I don't recall the exact quote, but it was roughly this:

> The moment you ship a project, you start stressing over traffic and metrics. That anxiety is as toxic as FOMO. All I can do is follow my curiosity, keep building, keep shipping, do whatever basic marketing and SEO I can, wait for time to give its feedback, and throw myself into the next project.

I think that's a strategy solo creators in the AI era ought to try.
