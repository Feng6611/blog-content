A lot has been going on lately.

First off, the Chinese New Year holiday is coming up. Work hasn't offered any real sense of accomplishment for months now. I've been coasting half the time, and that hollow emptiness brings an exhaustion that wears you down to the bone—I'm just waiting for the holiday break to save my skin.

On the other hand, I've really hit my stride with AI coding. Staying up late several nights in a row, I managed to knock out a few small plugins and features, including this blog. It's been deeply satisfying.

The first thing that really struck me was the intense feedback you get from working on a relatively well-defined problem.

When I tried using Next.js and Obsidian to build and write this blog, I noticed the mountain of dependencies was making Obsidian lag horribly. It's a crystal-clear scenario, and once you run into it, it's painful—and there was already plenty of discussion about it on the forums. So I built a plugin to fix it.

That feedback didn't even come from users yet. Just knowing I could solve a need that was sufficiently concrete and painful was already deeply satisfying.

Next is the sheer high of coding. Concrete tasks and clear boundaries make me feel tangibly alive.

I know you have to break things down into concrete steps to get them off the ground. But having been an INFP all these years, every bit of entropy in my life comes from wanting too much while being unable to break it down. Lately, I've been opening a fresh page in Apple Notes every day to write down my to-dos.

On days with a coding project, it's simple: write down the project -> list out the improvements -> check them off one by one. Done.

On days without one, my mind starts spinning: What do I want to do? What *could* I do? What matters more? If writing is more important, what should I write, and where do I even start? In the end, a lot of time slips away, and I usually just decide to read for thirty minutes and call it a day.

Just writing down these personal snippets is probably another symptom of my missing sense of the "concrete." Lately, I've been following a few people's blogs where they post daily notes—fragmented and messy, but really fun to read.

I figured I'd try writing a few myself. If I don't start getting things out, I'm going to suffocate.
